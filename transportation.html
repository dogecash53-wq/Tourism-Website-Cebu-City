Transportation and Travel Guide

How to get to Cebu

Cebu is the easiest major dive base in the Philippines to reach, and most travelers fly straight in. Mactan-Cebu International Airport sits on Mactan Island, a bridge away from Cebu City, and it takes direct flights from Manila and a long list of Asian cities — so for a lot of people, getting to Cebu is one flight and a short ride to the hotel. If you’re already in the Visayas, a fast ferry into the Cebu City pier is often the better move. This guide covers how to reach Cebu island itself: flying in from Manila or abroad, the overnight ferry, the fast craft from Bohol, Negros, Leyte, and Siquijor, and how to get from the airport into the city. By the end you’ll know which way in fits your budget and your timing, what each leg costs, and where to pick up the onward journey if you’re carrying on to Moalboal, Malapascua, or the southern coast.

Flying to Cebu: From Manila and Beyond

Flying is the fastest and usually the simplest way in. Mactan-Cebu is the busiest airport in the Visayas, so you have real choice on routes, times, and fares — and because Mactan is bridged to Cebu City, you don’t need an onward ferry or a long road transfer the way you do for most island dive towns

From Manila to Cebu

Manila–Cebu is one of the most-served domestic routes in the country, with dozens of departures a day across several carriers, so fares stay competitive and a late flight is rarely a problem.

Direct flight from NAIA

the default for almost everyone; you land 15 minutes from Cebu City by bridge, no boat involved. Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines, AirAsia, and Cebgo all fly the route.
  • Time: 1 hr 20 min–1 hr 30 min in the air
  • Cost: PHP 2,200–6,500 (USD $37–108), lowest when booked well ahead and outside peak weeks
    • Book early for the cheap fares — compare Manila to Cebu flights on Trip.com

    Flying from a Metro Manila address, build in a generous buffer to NAIA itself — cross-city traffic and the airport’s own peak-hour congestion swing your door-to-gate time more than the flight does.

    From other Philippine hubs to Cebu

    Cebu is a domestic hub in its own right, so you don’t have to backtrack through Manila to reach it. Direct flights run from Davao, Cagayan de Oro, Bacolod, Iloilo, Tacloban, and Siargao, plus seasonal routes from smaller airports — most are under an hour and a half.

    Direct domestic flight into Mactan-Cebu

    the one to take if you’re coming from Mindanao or the western Visayas and don’t want a Manila layover. Cebu Pacific, Cebgo, PAL, and AirAsia cover the main pairs.

    • Time: ~45 min–1 hr 30 min depending on origin
    • Cost: PHP 2,000–5,500 (~USD $33–92) typical, varies by route and how early you book
    • Find direct flights to Cebu on Trip.com for your origin and travel dates

    Direct international flights to Cebu

    You can skip Manila entirely. Mactan-Cebu takes direct international flights from a long list of Asian hubs — Singapore, Hong Kong, Seoul, Tokyo, Taipei, and more, with routes added and dropped season to season — which makes Cebu one of the few Philippine dive bases you can fly to without a domestic connection.

    If your home city has a direct route, it’s almost always worth taking over a Manila transfer: you clear immigration at Cebu, collect your bags once, and you’re a short ride from the city. Check current direct routes from your country with the airlines flying into Mactan-Cebu International Airport, and confirm whether your nationality needs anything beyond the standard visa-free entry before you book.

    Getting to Cebu by sea

    A boat to Cebu makes sense in two cases: you’re on a tight budget out of Manila and would rather sleep on the ferry than pay for a flight plus a hotel night, or you’re already somewhere in the Visayas and the crossing is short. Cebu City’s piers are the busiest ferry hub in the region, so the sea network is genuinely useful here, not a last resort.

    The 2GO overnight ferry from Manila

    2GO Travel from Manila the budget alternative to flying, and a slow but easy way to bring a lot of luggage (the generous baggage allowance suits divers hauling gear). Boats leave from Pier 4 at Manila’s North Harbour and dock at the Cebu City pier.

    • Time: 22–24 hours
    • Cost: PHP 2,200 (~USD $37) for a shared tourist-class bunk, up to PHP 8,600 (USD $143) for a private stateroom
    • Several sailings a week, most days of the week, on 2GO’s larger vessels — check the current 2GO sailing schedule for your date, and book the Manila to Cebu ferry on Bookaway or compare Manila to Cebu crossings on 12Go

    The crossing can get rough, especially in habagat (the southwest monsoon, June–October), when sailings are occasionally delayed or cancelled in heavy weather — so don’t book a tight onward connection the morning you’re due to dock.

    Fast ferries from around the Visayas

    If you’re already in the central Visayas, you fly or ferry into Cebu City rather than routing through Manila. Where there’s more than one way across, the options are labeled below; pick one.

    From Bohol — the shortest hop. OceanJet and other fast craft run frequently between Tagbilaran City Port and the Cebu City pier; the cheaper, slightly shorter crossing leaves from Tubigon Port.

    • Time: ~2 hrs from Tagbilaran; 1 hr 15 min from Tubigon
    • Cost: PHP 700–1,300 (USD $12–22)
    • Check Tagbilaran to Cebu ferry schedules on 2Go

    From Negros (Dumaguete)

    a longer fast-craft run, usually routed via Tagbilaran, or a quick Cebgo flight if you’d rather not spend the day at sea.

    • Time: 4–6 hrs by sea; 45 min by air
    • Cost: PHP 1,300–2,600 (USD $22–43) by ferry
    • Book the Dumaguete to Cebu ferry on Bookaway

    From Leyte (Ormoc)

    fast craft straight into the Cebu City pier, a common leg for travelers coming overland down Leyte.

    • Time: 3 hrs
    • Cost: PHP 900–1,800 (USD $15–30)
    • Compare Ormoc to Cebu crossings on 2Go

    From Siquijor

    there’s no fast craft straight to Cebu; the practical option is an overnight RORO from Larena Port run by Cokaliong or Lite Ferries, or a hop to Dumaguete first and a fast craft from there. The overnight boat isn’t sold through the usual booking apps for this leg, so confirm the current sailing direct with the operator.

    • Time: overnight (6–8 hrs)
    • Cost: PHP 900–1,500 (USD $15–25) for a bunk
    • Book at the Larena pier or through Cokaliong / Lite Ferries directly

    Fares and frequencies on these regulated multi-operator routes vary by operator, time of day, and demand, so treat the figures as indicative and confirm the live schedule for your travel date.

    From Mactan-Cebu airport to the city

    The airport is on Mactan Island, so unless your accommodation is on Mactan itself, you’ve got a bridge crossing into Cebu City. It’s a short hop, but the bridges off Mactan back up at rush hour — typically morning and late afternoon — so add a buffer if you’re connecting onward.

    Grab or a metered taxi

    door-to-door and the only round-the-clock option, so the one to use off a late flight. Open the Grab app for an upfront quote rather than taking a taxi from the rank — airport taxis here sometimes skip the meter and quote an inflated flat fare to anyone who looks unsure.

    • Time: 20–40 min to Cebu City; 10–20 min within Mactan
    • Cost: PHP 200–400 (~USD $3.30–6.70) to the city; PHP 150–250 (~USD $2.50–4.20) within Mactan — check the live Grab quote

    Bus airport shuttle

    the cheapest way off the airport if you’re light on luggage; it runs to SM City Cebu and SM Seaside in the city, where you can pick up a Grab or taxi for the last stretch.

    • Time: 30–60 min depending on traffic and stop
    • Cost: around PHP 50 (USD $0.80)
    • Runs through the day into the evening, not around the clock — so plan a Grab instead off a late arrival

    Already staying on Mactan, near the airport? Most resorts will arrange a pickup if you ask when you book — handy with dive gear and worth confirming the fare in advance.

    Onward from Cebu to the dive towns

    Reaching Cebu is rarely the end of the trip — the dive towns are spread the length of a long island, and each onward journey is its own leg with its own bus terminal and timing. This guide gets you to Cebu City and Mactan; the detail for each onward route lives in that destination’s guide.

    • Moalboal and the southwest coast
    buses and vans south from the Cebu South Bus Terminal, roughly three hours to Moalboal. The full route and fares are in how to get to Moalboal, and the sardine run, the Tañon Strait walls, and where to base are in the Moalboal dive guide.
    • Oslob and the far south
    also from the South Bus Terminal, about four hours to Oslob and Santander.
    • Malapascua in the north
    a four-to-five-hour run north from the Cebu North Bus Terminal to Maya Port, then a bangka (the local outrigger boat) across to the island.
    • Onward to Bohol, Negros, or Siquijor
    the same Cebu City fast-ferry network that brought you in runs the other way, making Cebu the natural hub for a multi-island Visayas trip.

    Planning your Cebu trip

    Cash, ATMs, and signal

    Cebu City and Mactan have ATMs, card-friendly hotels, and reliable mobile signal everywhere you’ll be while you’re in the metro. Draw cash before you head out to the smaller dive towns, where ATMs thin out and some operators take cash only. For data the moment you land, set up an eSIM before you fly — browse Philippine eSIM plans on Airalo or compare PH eSIM data plans on Klook so you can compare plans, then activate on arrival. A local Globe or Smart SIM at the airport works too if you’d rather buy on the ground.

    Travel insurance for the trip

    Ferry delays, a missed connection in habagat, a cancelled crossing, or a medical issue all land on you, not the operator. A travel policy that covers trip disruption and medical care takes the sting out of it — get SafetyWing trip-delay coverage for the whole trip, including the days you’re moving between islands.

    Weather and seasonal disruptions

    Cebu’s central position in the Visayas shelters it from the worst of both monsoons, so it loses fewer travel days to weather than more exposed regions. Even so, habagat (June–October) brings the typhoon window and the roughest seas — the crossings most likely to be delayed or suspended are the open-water ones (the Manila ferry, the longer Negros and Leyte runs). The calmer, drier amihan months (November–May) are the most reliable for sea travel. If your plan leans on a ferry, build in a buffer day in the wetter months and keep an eye on the operator’s advisories.
