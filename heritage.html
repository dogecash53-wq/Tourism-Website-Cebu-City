Landmarks of Faith

A City Rich in History

Cebu City holds an important place in Philippine history. It is associated with the arrival of Ferdinand Magellan in 1521 and the beginning of Spanish colonization in the Philippines. The city became an important center of trade, religion, and cultural exchange. Today, its historical landmarks and heritage sites preserve stories of the past and allow visitors to understand how Cebu developed into one of the country's major cities.

Basilica Minore del Santo Niño

The basilica houses the image of the Santo Niño, given to Rajah Humabon’s queen in 1521. Pilgrims from all over the country visit, especially in January.

Magellan’s Cross

The kiosk beside the basilica protects a cross said to enclose the original one planted by the Spaniards. Its ceiling shows scenes of the first baptisms.

Fort San Pedro

Fort San Pedro began as a defensive structure for the first Spanish settlement in Cebu. The present stone structure takes the form of a triangular bastioned fort—coral-stone walls, vintage cannons, a garden courtyard, and elevated views from the ramparts.

Casa Gorordo Museum

From the 1850s, the Gorordo House witnessed the lives of four generations of the family, including Juan Gorordo, the first Filipino bishop of Cebu. Remarkably, this historic residence survived two revolutions in the late 1800s and early 1900s and withstood the ravages of the Second World War.

Simala Shrine

Over the years, the Simala Shrine has seen significant development. Initially a modest structure, the church has transformed into a grand, castle-like building. The architecture aims to inspire awe and devotion among visitors. The site now includes extensive facilities for prayers and gatherings, making it a focal point for pilgrims in Cebu.
